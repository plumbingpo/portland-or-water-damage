Of all the stages in restoring water damage, drying the structure counts most, and it is the stage most often botched by people without training. Extraction takes care of standing water, but drywall, framing, subflooring, insulation, concrete and other building materials stay loaded with trapped moisture. A structure left wet ends up with odors, wood rot, mold and failing materials, so the moisture has to be removed in a scientific way using dehumidification and controlled evaporation.

The structural drying technicians at Portland Restoration Co are IICRC-certified and trained in psychrometry, the science of how temperature, humidity and moisture in air relate. That knowledge lets them work out where equipment goes, what temperature to hold and how air should move so evaporation is as effective as possible on every job. Different materials (drywall, hardwood, concrete, plaster) dry at different rates and need different approaches to reach acceptable moisture levels without causing additional damage.

Our structural drying process includes daily moisture monitoring with calibrated moisture meters, thermo-hygrometers and thermal imaging cameras. Readings are recorded from start to finish in a detailed drying log, which shows moisture dropping step by step in every affected material. Each day we use those numbers to reposition or retune the equipment so drying stays efficient. Drying is complete only when all affected materials reach acceptable moisture standards as defined by IICRC S500 guidelines, not when surfaces simply feel dry to the touch.

Our Structural Drying Process

Our systematic approach delivers quality results on every project. Here's how we handle structural drying from start to finish:

1

Mapping the Moisture

We build a complete moisture map of every affected area with hygrometers, thermal imaging and moisture meters. Baseline readings come from the map, and so does the plan for placing equipment.

2

Equipment Deployment

Guided by the moisture map, we place high-velocity air movers and commercial LGR dehumidifiers where they will do the most good. We tune airflow and temperature to suit the materials that need drying.

3

Daily Monitoring

Our technician returns daily to take moisture readings at all monitoring points, adjust equipment placement and document drying progress. Along the way, we tell you where the drying schedule stands.

4

Verifying the Dry Standard

When moisture readings reach acceptable levels throughout all affected materials, we document final readings, remove equipment and certify that your property has reached dry standard.

What a Professional Brings to Structural Drying

There is real science behind structural drying, which is why guessing does not work. The leading cause of secondary mold after a water event is setting out a few fans and trusting they will be enough. It involves daily monitoring, a calculation of the required grain depression (the capacity to remove moisture) and a working knowledge of the way materials absorb moisture and let it go. Get it wrong in one direction and mold feeds on leftover moisture, get it wrong in the other and wood or similar materials are damaged by over-drying.

The equipment used for professional structural drying is fundamentally different from consumer products. Commercial LGR (low-grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers remove 10 to 20 times more moisture from the air than residential dehumidifiers, and high-velocity air movers create the precise airflow patterns needed to accelerate evaporation from structural surfaces. Between the investment in equipment and the training needed to run it, it is no surprise that DIY drying falls well short of a professional job.

Maintenance Tips for Structural Drying

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Air movers need a clear path, so avoid stacking things on the equipment or in front of it
  • Let air movers and dehumidifiers run continuously, 24/7, until the technician removes them
  • Dehumidifiers lose efficiency when outside air gets in, so keep drying areas closed up
  • Make sure the technician can get in each day for monitoring visits
  • Avoid moving the machines yourself, because their placement follows our moisture map
  • Go by the meter, not by touch, since a dry-feeling surface may still hold moisture deep inside

How to Tell You Need Structural Drying

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Swollen trim and baseboards separating at the seams
  • Stained, swollen or soft spots in the drywall
  • Hardwood floors that are cupping, crowning or showing gaps between boards
  • Beads of moisture on windows and cold surfaces after the water event
  • Surfaces that stay cool or clammy once the standing water has been removed
  • Humidity that will not come down in the affected space, whatever ventilation you try
  • Earlier water damage that was never professionally dried and now shows deterioration
