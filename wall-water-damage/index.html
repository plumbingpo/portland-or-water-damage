Wall and Drywall Water Damage Help in Portland

Drywall acts like a wick. Once the base of a wall gets wet, moisture climbs through the paper and gypsum, commonly a foot or more higher than the water stood on the floor. From the room, the surface may seem dry and sound even though the hidden face and the insulation are saturated. That is why we check walls with a moisture meter instead of trusting how they look.

Plenty of walls that get wet can stay. Firm panels wetted by clean water are frequently saved, in some cases with the baseboard taken off and small openings drilled so air can move inside the wall. When the wall is soft, crumbling, moldy or was soaked by dirty water, we make a flood cut, a straight line above the damage, and remove everything below it.

Warning Signs

  • Trim along the floor that has puffed up, twisted or come loose
  • Wet tack strip, lifting planks or stained flooring at the base of the wall
  • Wall coverings lifting, blistering or creasing close to floor level
  • Soft or crumbly spots you notice when pushing lightly on the wall
  • Drywall seams ridging up and screw pops appearing across the surface
  • Speckled growth showing up where furniture sits against the wall or at corners
Our Process

How We Handle Wall and Drywall Water Damage

1

Find Every Wet Area

Every wall is checked with a thermal camera and moisture meter, and we mark the height and reach of the water, covering the other face and the rooms next door.

2

Sort Save From Remove

Our call on each section, dry it or take it out, depends on water category, exposure time and the condition of the panel, and we explain it in plain terms.

3

Open Up or Air Out

For walls being saved, baseboards come off and air is directed into the cavity. On sections coming out, a straight flood cut is made and soaked insulation is removed.

4

Dry and Rebuild

Air movers and dehumidifiers run until studs and remaining drywall read dry, typically 3 to 5 days. After that comes insulation, new drywall hung and finished, trim put back and paint.

The Damage Timeline

First hours

Water Starts Climbing

Moisture enters where the panel meets the floor and wicks upward from there. Both the baseboard and the bottom plate start absorbing water.

Hours 24 to 48

Wall Swells

Moisture keeps climbing, the baseboards puff up and blisters form in the paint. Inside the cavity, the paper face becomes a place mold can begin.

Days 3 through 7

Drywall Breaks Down

Paper peels from a core that is no longer firm. A musty odor grows stronger while the batts settle and mold covers more of the unseen face.

After a week

Damage Reaches the Studs

Studs and plates stay wet long enough to grow mold and begin to decay. A far larger section of wall needs tearing out and rebuilding.

What You Cannot See

The Damage You Do Not See

Wet Sheathing Outside

Outside walls have sheathing past the insulation that can also get soaked. Because it is slow to dry, we test it before any new drywall goes up.

Walls You Cannot See

Sections of wall covered by cabinets, a vanity or shelving stay damp because air never reaches them. We take readings through toe kicks and from the opposite side to find it.

Wet Outlets and Wiring

Outlets and wiring are mounted near the floor, exactly where the water gathers. Do not use outlets on a wet wall until the circuit has been checked.

Water Next Door

Water passes under the bottom plate into the next room or closet. Our team inspects each wet wall from both sides, plus the floor running beside it.
