Getting the moisture out covers just part of the work. Once the moisture readings are back to normal, there are still cut walls, missing flooring and bare subfloor to deal with. Water damage repair is the rebuild. For Portland homeowners, our crew installs replacements for anything unsalvageable and blends them with the existing finishes until the space stops looking like a job site.

Repair work covers a wide range. A minor loss can need just a couple of feet of wallboard plus baseboard and a coat of paint. On a large one it can mean insulation, full sheets of drywall, subfloor, hardwood or laminate, cabinet boxes, trim and doors. Our aim is a like-for-like replacement, and when a true match cannot be found we say so directly and let you decide.

Nothing gets closed up until it is dry. Ahead of installing anything new, our team meters the framing and subfloor to verify normal moisture. Water Damage Repair Prevention Tips

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Give fresh paint, caulk and flooring adhesive time to cure before wet mopping or heavy use.
  • A water stain should stay unpainted and unpatched while its cause is still active.
  • Use moisture resistant drywall and backer board when rebuilding bathrooms, kitchens and laundry rooms.
  • No wall or floor should be sealed by any contractor until you have seen final moisture readings.
  • Rebuilt bathroom walls last longer when the exhaust fan runs through and after each shower.
  • Keep leftover flooring, tile and paint details so future repairs can be matched.

How to Tell You Need Water Damage Repair

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Drywall was cut out during drying and the wall cavities are still open.
  • The lower part of cabinet sides or bases feels soft, looks swollen or is peeling.
  • Tiles sound hollow, have come loose or show cracked grout since water got beneath them.
  • A leak overhead left the ceiling drywall cracked, sagging or falling apart.
  • Interior doors now stick or will not latch after swelling from moisture.
  • Walking over the formerly wet area, you feel soft spots in the floor.
  • Where water ran down a wall, the paint has blistered, peeled or turned brown.
