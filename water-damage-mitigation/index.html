Put simply, mitigation is the work of holding damage to its current size. Do not confuse it with rebuilding. It is everything done before the repair to protect what is still sound. Our Portland crew mitigates a loss in four moves: control the source, extract the water, contain the wet zone and stabilize the building. Good mitigation makes the later repair smaller, and now and then makes it unnecessary.

The words mitigation and restoration get used as if they were interchangeable. In fact there are two separate stages. During mitigation, the urgent part, the loss is halted, its spread is limited and the home is made stable. Restoration is what comes after: drying is confirmed, and damaged drywall, flooring and finishes are repaired or rebuilt. Because an insurer lists each one separately as well, knowing where you stand in the process is helpful.

On a standard mitigation call we work through four items. One: control the source and cut off any new water. Extraction, so standing water is gone. Containment, using plastic barriers and controlled airflow so moisture and contaminants stay out of clean rooms. Fourth is stabilization: unsalvageable materials come out, surfaces receive antimicrobial treatment, and dehumidifiers and air movers are placed. Along the way, your belongings and furniture get moved or set up on blocks to keep them safe.

Why Hire a Professional for Water Damage Mitigation?

Mitigation is a series of judgment calls made quickly. Can this section of drywall dry in place, or does it need cutting out? Is the water Category 1, 2 or 3, and has it changed since the leak began? Does the cabinet toe kick need to come off? We answer those questions with training and moisture readings rather than by the look or feel of a surface.

Your insurance claim benefits from proper mitigation too. Most policies require the owner to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage, and insurers want proof it was done. Arrival photos, daily moisture readings and an inventory of removed materials all go into our records. Together those records show the water's effect and our response to it. The repair contractor gets a clear place to start as well, without hidden moisture waiting behind fresh drywall.

When to Call for Water Damage Mitigation in Portland

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Following a water loss, indoor air stays damp and condensation forms on the glass.
  • The carpet pad is soaked through even where the surface seems only damp.
  • Flooring is carrying moisture past the doorway to an adjoining room.
  • Water is no longer flowing, though it has left wet floors, cabinets or walls behind.
  • Plumbing repairs are done, but no one has addressed what got wet.
  • Wet materials have sat for a day and a musty smell is starting.
  • An adjuster or agent said you must reasonably limit additional damage.

Water Damage Mitigation, Step by Step

Our systematic approach delivers quality results on every project. Here's what you can expect when you choose Portland Restoration Co:

1

Stop the Water Source

Depending on the cause, our crew closes the supply, caps a failed line, or covers the entry point with tarps and boards. If a plumber or roofer is needed for the permanent fix, we coordinate the timing.

2

Remove Standing Water

Standing water is removed with pumps and extractors, and carpet and pad are checked. Getting bulk water out quickly does more to limit damage than any amount of drying equipment alone.

3

Set Up Containment

Wet rooms are closed off from dry ones with barriers of plastic sheeting and controlled airflow. This keeps humidity, dust and any contaminants from travelling through the house while work is under way.

4

Stabilize and Dry

Materials past saving come out, an antimicrobial is applied to surfaces, and air movers plus dehumidifiers go in. Moisture readings are recorded on every visit until the structure can be repaired, typically in 3 to 5 days.

How to Prevent Water Damage Mitigation Problems

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Have your water heater inspected yearly and replace it before the tank fails.
  • Keep gutters clear and downspouts pointed away from the foundation of the house.
  • Leave the heat on during winter trips so the plumbing cannot freeze.
  • Replace rubber washing machine hoses with braided steel lines every five years or so.
  • Have an automatic shutoff fitted, so the main closes itself once a leak is sensed.
  • A main shutoff that will not turn is no help, so locate and test yours.
